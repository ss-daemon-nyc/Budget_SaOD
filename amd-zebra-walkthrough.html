The shape

A three-leg zebra, and a weekly sold beside it

The zebra is a standalone three-leg order: two long calls and one short, all in March 2027. Same cycle is not a preference — the short only cancels the longs’ time value if it sits in the same expiration.

The weekly is not part of that order. It is sold on its own, and the broker pairs it against the zebra’s spare long call overnight, which is what turns it into a covered diagonal. So this is a three-leg position plus a separate weekly sale — not a four-leg spread. The practical consequence lives in the gap: between the sale and the overnight pairing, the weekly can price as an uncovered short.

Buy ×2  /  core2 × AMD 380 CITM, ~77Δ each. These two are the engine — they supply the ~100 deltas, and they carry the position past the late-February close of the window.
Sell ×1  /  core1 × AMD 455 CThe true ATM strike — which at this tenor is ~60Δ, not 50. Its fat extrinsic pays for the extrinsic in both longs. That is the whole trick.
Sell ×1  /  weekly, separate1 × AMD 520 CSold separately each week, then paired to the spare long overnight. Roll it through the flat stretch, then back off once the window opens.
Net result≈ 88Δ  ·  defined riskBehaves like long stock between the strikes, but the loss stops at the debit paid.

Naming: ZEBRA = Zero Extrinsic Back Ratio, coined on tastytrade. The “zero extrinsic” is the design target, not a guarantee — it holds at entry, with the short leg genuinely at the money, and it drifts as soon as AMD moves.

The trade-off nobody mentions

Zero extrinsic and 100 deltas pull against each other. Driving net extrinsic to zero wants your long strike shallower; driving net delta to 1.00 wants it deeper. You cannot always have both, and chasing delta by going deeper ITM quietly raises your debit — which is your max loss. When they conflict, hold the zero-extrinsic line and accept 0.88–0.95 deltas. Extrinsic is the thing that bleeds; a few deltas are not.

The weekly decision

Where to sell, and why that level and not the one beside it

The zebra is entered once. The weekly is a decision you make again every Monday, so it needs a method rather than a number. Feed it last week’s range and your moving averages; it derives the Fibonacci extensions, the one-sigma move, and shows you where they stack up. Sell above the confluence, never into it.

      Failure modes
      
What actually goes wrong, ranked by how often

    

    

      

        
RiskSeverityHow it shows upWhat removes it

        
          

            The gap before the overnight pairingHigh
            The weekly is sold standalone, so until the broker pairs it against your spare long it is an uncovered short call. Buying power drops by a lot rather than rising by the credit, and it stays that way until the pairing runs.
            Expected behaviour, not a malfunction — but confirm the timing at Gate 3 and keep enough buying power to carry the weekly uncovered overnight. The danger is not the pairing; it is touching a long leg while a weekly sits unpaired.
          

          

            Overlay outlives the coreBlocking
            You sell an overlay dated past the core’s expiration. The longs expire, the short does not, and you are outright naked short AMD calls.
            A hard rule with no exceptions: the overlay’s expiration is always earlier than or equal to the core’s.
          

          

            Gap through the capHigh
            AMD prints a number and moves 9% overnight — it did exactly that on Q2 2026 earnings, in the losing direction despite a beat. Your overlay is deep ITM by the open and cannot be rolled for a credit.
            Decide deliberately whether an overlay cycle spans an earnings date. Sizing the overlay at 20–25Δ rather than 35Δ buys real room.
          

          

            Policy and tariff headlinesHigh
            Semiconductor tariff proposals move the whole complex at once, and the ones under discussion reach past chips into finished goods that contain them — laptops, consoles, data-centre servers. Unlike earnings, these land on no schedule, so no overlay cycle can be planned around them.
            Nothing removes it; it is the cost of holding a semi for two quarters. What it argues for is sizing that survives a headline gap, and not treating a quiet tape as low risk just because no catalyst is on the calendar.
          

          

            Delta melts on the way downMedium
            AMD falls toward your long strike; the position stops tracking the stock because the long calls are shedding delta. It does not participate in the bounce the way stock would.
            Understand it as designed behaviour, not a malfunction — it is the same mechanism that caps your loss. Set your “close or hold” rule before entry, at Gate 6.
          

          

            Extrinsic creeps back inMedium
            The short leg stops being at the money, the offset stops balancing, and the position starts carrying real theta in one direction or the other.
            Re-check the gauge periodically. Adjusting the core mid-life is usually worse than closing and rebuilding it cleanly.
          

          

            Expiration-week assignment scrambleMedium
            Both core legs finish ITM, auto-exercise fires, and Monday morning you own an unplanned share position — possibly on margin.
            Close or roll the core at 21–30 DTE. Do not carry a 2–1 ratio into expiration week to save a few cents of slippage.
          

          

            Early assignment on a short callLow
            You are assigned short 100 shares against your longs before expiration.
            Structurally low here: the usual trigger is a dividend capture, and AMD has not historically paid one. Confirm that is still true rather than assuming it.
          

        
      

    

  


  

    Standing assumptions
    
Built for a bullish core view on AMD over the life of the chosen expiration, using calls throughout. Every figure is per-share unless a dollar total is labelled otherwise; one contract represents 100 shares. Commissions, assignment fees, and bid–ask slippage are excluded and are not trivial — three legs on entry, then again on every weekly sale and roll, which at a weekly cadence is where they accumulate fastest. Nothing here is personalised advice; the numbers are yours to verify against your own chain and your own account.
